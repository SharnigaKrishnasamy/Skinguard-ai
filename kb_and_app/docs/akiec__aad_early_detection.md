\# Actinic Keratosis: Changes to Watch For



\## Overview

Actinic keratosis is a skin change commonly linked to long-term ultraviolet radiation exposure. It is important to pay attention to skin areas that change over time.



\## Common observations

\- The affected area may feel rough, dry, or scaly.

\- A patch may be easier to feel than to see.

\- Lesions often occur on sun-exposed areas, including the face, ears, scalp, forearms, and hands.

\- Skin changes can look similar to other conditions, so appearance alone is not enough to identify the cause.



\## Monitoring and prevention

Protecting skin from excessive ultraviolet exposure is an important general skin-health measure. Protective clothing, shade, and broad-spectrum sunscreen can help reduce exposure. These measures do not replace professional assessment of an existing lesion.



\## When to seek professional assessment

Ask a qualified healthcare professional to assess a lesion that is new, changing, persistent, tender, or bleeding. Do not rely on an AI image prediction to determine whether a skin lesion is precancerous or cancerous.
